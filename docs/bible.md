# Character Bible — On the Rocks: The Last Shift

Dated 2026-11-01. Frank Sable and The Voice predate the jam (from the author's unreleased series *On the Rocks*). Everyone else, the bar, and the case were created for Game Off 2026.

---

## Frank Sable — the detective

- Late 40s. Ex-police, now "consulting," which means nobody consults him.
- Drinks on a strict 90-minute schedule. He calls it discipline. It's maintenance.
- **Sober Frank** is bumbling, literal, polite to a fault, apologises to furniture. He sees surfaces: what's there, what's missing, what people say. He takes everyone at their word because he's embarrassed not to.
- **Frank with The Voice** is razor-sharp, fast, cruel when it's useful, and reckless. He sees subtext: why the glass is sweating, why the lie was that specific. He also loses time. Memory from Voice windows comes back in fragments, if at all.
- Self-aware. Frank knows what he is. He's just stopped arguing about it.
- Tell: rubs his right knuckles when he's lying to himself.

**Sober voice sample:** "Right. Okay. So. A dead man. That's — yes. I should probably not touch that."

## The Voice — the second narrator

- Not a hallucination, not a demon, not explained. It arrives with the second drink and leaves with the sobriety.
- Speaks in second person, to Frank, in italics. Witty, surgical, never kind, always correct — except once.
- The Voice's job in the series is to make Frank brilliant. Its job in this game is to protect Frank from one memory. It will lie by omission to do it. That is the twist.
- The Voice never says "I." It says "you."

**Voice sample:** *You've been looking at that bottle for forty seconds. It's the wrong bottle. He doesn't stock it. Somebody brought it down from upstairs, and you already know who sleeps upstairs.*

## The bar — THE LAST CALL

Corner bar, two rooms and an office, apartment upstairs. Closes at midnight. Cash till, no cameras ("the camera's a sticker, Frank"). Brass bar rail. One top-shelf bottle nobody's allowed to touch.

Locations in game: **Booth** (where Frank wakes), **Bar floor**, **Behind the bar**, **Office**, **Back door / alley**.

---

## The victim — DANNY ROARKE

- Owner-bartender, 50s, big, tired, decent. Six weeks behind on paying his barback, three months behind on paying his partner. Keeps a handwritten tab book. Cuts Frank off every night at 11:45 like a ritual; Frank hates him for it and loves him for it.
- Found behind the bar. Head wound. Broken bottle beside him.
- **How he actually died:** an epidural bleed from hitting the brass rail. He was conscious for forty minutes after. Three people saw him alive on that floor and left.

---

## Suspects

### Mira Roarke — the wife
- 40s. Co-owner, does the books, lives upstairs. Composed to the point of being frightening.
- **Claims:** she was upstairs with the accounts, came down at 12:45, found him, called no one until 1:30 "because I couldn't move."
- **Truth:** she came down at 12:45, found Danny dead, the till empty, the ledger gone. She assumed robbery. She knew the bar's insurance pays on a crime and not on a drunk man falling. She broke the top-shelf bottle over the body to sell it, wiped the rail, and waited.
- **Sober reads:** grieving, cooperative. **Voice reads:** her hands are clean and the rail is cleaner.
- Guilt: staging a crime scene. Not murder.

### Theo Pike — the barback
- 22. Owed six weeks' wages. Left at midnight, "came back for my phone" at 12:10.
- **Claims:** grabbed his phone, left, saw nothing.
- **Truth:** found Danny on the floor, dazed, who told him to get out. Theo took $1,800 from the till — exactly what he was owed — and left Danny there because he was angry and scared. His phone is still on the charger behind the bar; he never came back for it. He came back for the money.
- **Sober reads:** scared kid. **Voice reads:** the phone.
- Guilt: theft, and leaving a man who needed help.

### Leonard "Lenny" Baptiste — the partner
- 60s. "Silent" partner, which means he's loud about it. Danny owed him. Smokes cigars he's not allowed to smoke inside.
- **Claims:** arrived at 12:30 to "talk," door was locked, left.
- **Truth:** door was open. Found Danny on the floor, slurring. Went into the office, took the deed and the ledger — the paper that proved the debt — and left. Didn't call anyone because a dead Danny with no paperwork is a bar that belongs to Lenny.
- **Sober reads:** blustering, offended. **Voice reads:** cigar ash on the office carpet, and he said "was" before anyone told him Danny was dead.
- Guilt: theft, abandonment, and the closest thing to intent in the room.

### Constance "Connie" Vale — the regular
- 70s. Retired teacher. Sits on the last stool every night. Says she left at midnight.
- **Claims:** went home at midnight, came back at 1:30 when she "had a feeling."
- **Truth:** she never left. She was on her stool in the dark the whole time, drunk, and she saw all of it — Frank's shove, Theo at the till, Lenny in the office, Mira with the bottle. She said nothing because she likes Frank, and because at her age she's learned that the truth doesn't fix anything.
- **Sober reads:** sweet, vague. **Voice reads:** her glass is still sweating. She's been here the whole time.
- Guilt: none in law. She's the witness. She's the key to the true ending.

## What Frank did

- 11:45 PM: Danny cuts Frank off. Writes it on the receipt like always: "11:47 — CUT OFF. — D."
- 11:50 PM: Frank, deep in a Voice window, argues. The Voice says something unforgivable about Danny's marriage. Danny grabs Frank's collar. Frank shoves him. Danny goes backward over the rail, hits his head on the brass, sits down hard, says "get out of my bar, Frank." Frank goes to his booth and passes out.
- 1:40 AM: Frank wakes. Sober. No memory of 11:50.

The Voice remembers. The Voice is not going to tell him.

## The true ending

Frank assembles the timeline from both states and corners the Voice in Chapter 6. The Voice admits it. Then the game asks Frank the series' question: tell Mira, Theo, Lenny and Connie the truth and take the first push — or let Mira's robbery story stand, let Lenny keep the bar, and have a drink.

Both endings are playable. Neither is scored as "correct." The game remembers which one you picked.
